# Vhaezunoryth

> A porcelain stag drinks the darkness left inside a broken hour.

## The Courtyard of Quiet Antlers

The stag stands in the courtyard of a house whose inhabitants have never been seen. Its body is white porcelain. Its antlers are branches of dark glass, and between them hangs a clock without a face.

Rain leaves no mark on the stag. Dust avoids its hooves. On summer mornings, a shallow pool appears beneath its muzzle, though the courtyard stones remain dry around it. The water is black and reflects the clock as if it possessed hands.

The housekeeper comes at dusk. She wears a green veil and carries a brush made of silver grass. She brushes the stag from shoulder to tail, always leaving a narrow strip untouched along its spine. That strip grows darker every evening.

When asked who lives in the house, she opens her empty hand. Those who look into it remember a room they have never entered and immediately wish to leave it.

## An Hour Found on the Road

A traveler once reached the courtyard carrying a broken hour in a wooden box. He had found it beside a milestone on a road that became shorter whenever he looked behind him. The hour consisted of two curved fragments and a fine powder smelling of extinguished candles.

The housekeeper examined the box. She touched neither fragment, but asked whether the traveler had heard anything while carrying them. He said that at night something had walked around his bed, pausing whenever he tried to count its steps.

She placed the box beneath the stag. The pool appeared at once. Its black water rose over the rim of the box without wetting the wood. One fragment sank. The other remained suspended, turning slowly beneath the stag's porcelain muzzle.

Inside the house, a door closed. The traveler had heard no door open.

## The Rooms That Borrow Their Walls

There are thirteen windows overlooking the courtyard. Each reveals the same room from a different angle. The room contains a table, a chair, and a painting of a forest in which every tree leans toward something beyond the frame.

Sometimes the chair appears beside the table. Sometimes it stands on the ceiling. On evenings when the stag's untouched strip is darkest, the chair is occupied by a figure wearing the housekeeper's veil.

The housekeeper denies sitting there. She says the room borrows its walls from a house that no longer has an inside. Its occupant is waiting for those walls to be returned.

The traveler counted the windows from left to right and found thirteen. Counting from right to left, he found twelve. He tried once more and saw a small brass door between the second and third windows. Behind it came the sound of a forest growing in a place too narrow for trees.

## Under the Porcelain

A crack runs beneath the stag's chest. It is visible only in the reflection of the pool. Through it shines a deep amber light, interrupted at intervals by something that resembles a slow heartbeat.

The traveler knelt beside the water. Beneath the reflected crack he saw a scaled neck, then the edge of a folded wing. The stag's porcelain body was no larger than an ordinary animal, but the creature within it continued beyond the edges of the courtyard.

Its antlers entered the forest in the painting. Its tail lay beneath the distant milestone. Its sleeping eye occupied the faceless clock, which now seemed to be hanging not between glass branches but between two enormous claws.

The traveler stood quickly. The clock swung once. In the wooden box, the remaining fragment of the hour turned toward him like a face.

## The Veil's Inner Seam

That night, the housekeeper removed her veil and placed it over the box. Embroidered along its inner seam were four groups of letters:

*Vhae — zu — noryth — eshal.*

The traveler recognized the handwriting but could not place it. He remembered seeing the same marks on the milestone, on the frame of a childhood mirror, and on the underside of a chair that had once stood in his mother's room.

The housekeeper asked him which memory came first. He pointed to the mirror. She unfolded the veil, and the box beneath it became transparent. Inside, the two fragments had joined around a small hollow filled with green light.

He heard the footsteps from his nights on the road. This time they were moving away.

## What the Stag Refused

At dawn, the traveler offered the joined hour to the stag. The porcelain muzzle lowered until it nearly touched his hand. The dark glass antlers shone, and the painting in the upper windows lost all its trees.

The stag did not drink. Instead, the pool emptied upward, becoming a thin ribbon of black water that entered the faceless clock. The traveler felt something warm settle inside his chest, in a place no hand could reach.

The housekeeper returned his wooden box. It held a single porcelain leaf. She brushed the stag and, for the first time, passed the silver grass across the strip along its spine.

When the traveler reached the road, the milestone had moved. Its distance was written as a word he could not pronounce.

Behind him, the courtyard remained dry.

The clock remained without a face.

But in the empty painting, one small tree had begun to cast a shadow.
